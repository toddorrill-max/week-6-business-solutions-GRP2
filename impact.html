Spend less time copying numbers between spreadsheets

The idea is simple: first find out where every important spreadsheet gets its numbers. Then automate the reports that consume the most staff time. A 12-week trial limits the risk before the company approves the larger project.

In plain English

What the customer gains

Today, employees spend thousands of hours preparing spreadsheets and answering requests for the same information. The proposed solution creates reports directly from approved company data, so people can spend more time using the numbers and less time moving them around.

Less manual workEmployees spend fewer hours copying, linking, and reconciling spreadsheet data.
More confidenceReports use agreed data sources instead of long chains of workbook links.
Lower riskThe company tests the idea for 12 weeks before approving the larger spend.
Clear ownershipEach report has a named data owner, refresh schedule, and support path.

The simple financial story: in the base estimate, the project costs $786K over three years and removes about $1.017M of manual work. That leaves an estimated $231K more benefit than cost. The estimate assumes employees actually stop doing the duplicate spreadsheet work after the automated reports launch.

The control point

After 12 weeks, leaders choose whether to continue

The first phase creates a list of the spreadsheets, shows where their numbers come from, and identifies reports that can be automated. Leaders approve the remaining budget only when all three checks pass.

90%

Most spreadsheets were checked

The approved scanning software reads at least 90% of the spreadsheets on the list.

70%

A manageable set of data sources matters most

A small group of shared sources supplies at least 70% of the repeated spreadsheet links.

50%

Enough manual work can be replaced

People responsible for the data confirm that automated reports can replace at least 50% of today’s effort.

Stop condition

If any check fails, do not approve the larger project. Keep the spreadsheet list, the map showing where the numbers come from, the owner comments, and the unresolved issues for a smaller future effort.

Implementation playbook

Nine steps another team can follow

Give every task a named owner. Save the scan results and owner approvals so anyone can see why leaders decided to stop or continue.

  1. Charter and fund Phase 1

    The sponsor and PMO define scope, access rules, decision rights, the $90K cap, and the week-12 review date.

    OutputSigned charter, budget, owners, and calendar

  2. Make a list of the spreadsheets

    Business teams record what each active spreadsheet does, how often people use it, who owns it, where it is stored, and how important it is. Mark duplicates, but keep a record of them.

    OutputControlled inventory with a unique ID for every workbook

  3. Check formulas, links, and source files

    Use approved software to read formulas and identify where each spreadsheet gets its numbers. Record files that cannot be opened or need a person to check them.

    OutputMachine-readable link list and exception log

  4. Show where the numbers come from

    Group spreadsheets that use the same data source. Put the sources used by the most reports and consuming the most staff time at the top.

    OutputSource-to-workbook map with concentration totals

  5. Ask the data owners to confirm the map

    For each source, confirm what the data means, who owns it, when it updates, and whether an automatic report can replace the manual spreadsheet task.

    OutputApproved source map and replacement-path register

  6. Hold the stop or proceed review

    Calculate the three gate measures from the retained evidence. Proceed only when every threshold passes and the sponsor accepts the remaining cost and risk.

    OutputDated decision record with pass or fail evidence

  7. Build automatic reports

    Create reports that refresh on a schedule from approved company data. Start with the reports that remove the most repeated spreadsheet work.

    OutputTested reports, schedules, access controls, and support plan

  8. Run the old and new reports side by side

    For one quarterly reporting cycle, compare the automatic reports with the current spreadsheets. Investigate important differences and obtain approval before switching.

    OutputReconciliation record and signed control approval

  9. Train, go live, and retire duplicates

    Train report owners and users, publish support contacts, archive approved records, and remove duplicate workbook processes only after the new reports meet acceptance criteria.

    OutputLive reports, trained users, and retirement log

How the estimate was calculated: the company starts with 1,000 spreadsheets per quarter. At 4.25 hours each, over four quarters, the work totals about 17,000 hours. Multiplying those hours by $57 in pay and benefits gives about $969K per year.

Base-case estimate: a savings ramp of 15%, 40%, and 50% produces $1.017M in three-year benefits against $786K in three-year costs.

Decision quality

Assumptions, limitations, and the next improvement

Model assumptions

  • 1,000 workbooks per quarter represents the active workload.
  • Each workbook consumes 4.25 hours, including requests and reconciliation.
  • Pay plus employee benefits averages $57 per hour, based on wage and benefit benchmarks.
  • The one-time cost is $561K and the annual run cost after launch is $90K.
  • Labor savings reach 15%, 40%, and 50% over the first three years.

Known limitations

  • The cost range uses bottom-up staffing estimates rather than vendor quotes.
  • The $120 hourly technology planning rate combines employee and outside-specialist costs and still needs to be checked.
  • The model excludes correction of errors, retired storage, and added management review time.
  • Protected files, undocumented manual steps, and owner availability may reduce scan coverage.
  • Estimated savings become real only when teams retire duplicate workbook work.

Realistic next improvement

Add a measured pilot before finalizing the full business case

During Phase 1, select 25–50 typical spreadsheets from the most-used data source. Measure how long the current work takes, manually check the scan, build one automatic report, and record the hours it actually saves. Use those results to replace the 4.25-hour estimate, check the $120 technology rate, and update the savings forecast before the week-12 review.
